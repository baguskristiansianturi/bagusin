# Bagusin — Article Editorial & SEO SOP

**Status:** Mandatory editorial standard for planning, researching, writing, reviewing and publishing Bagusin articles.  
**Applies to:** All Bagusin articles, including the planned 50-article content programme, future updates, translations and derivative formats.  
**Primary rule:** If an article cannot meet the originality, evidence, usefulness and quality requirements below, do not publish it yet.

## 1. Editorial principles

- Write for the reader's real question first. Search visibility is important, but must not override truth, usefulness, clarity or originality.
- Use natural, friendly, respectful and professional Indonesian. Avoid robotic phrasing, filler, inflated claims, keyword stuffing and unexplained jargon.
- Explain technical terms when a beginner needs the explanation. Use examples that clarify the idea rather than imitate a source's examples.
- Be honest about uncertainty, limitations, changing features and what Bagusin has or has not personally tested.
- Free instructional articles should provide complete, useful help for the task they promise. Do not intentionally omit essential steps to force a purchase.
- If a topic cannot be covered responsibly or distinctly, pause it, narrow its scope, combine it with a more suitable page, or reject it. Publishing is not mandatory.

## 2. Originality: non-negotiable

“100% original” means the article's wording, explanation, structure, examples, reasoning, framing and presentation are independently created for Bagusin—not copied or mechanically transformed from another page.

Prohibited:
- Copying or lightly editing sentences, paragraphs, headings, tables, examples or distinctive formulations from another publisher.
- Translating a source and presenting the translation as original.
- Rewriting a source paragraph by paragraph, following its distinctive outline too closely, or replacing a few words with synonyms.
- Combining copied snippets from multiple sources into an article.
- Inventing personal experience, tests, interviews, data, quotes or expertise that Bagusin does not have.
- Treating an AI-detector score or a plagiarism tool's “pass” as proof of originality.

Required method:
1. Define the reader's question and the article's unique purpose before researching.
2. Research sources to understand and verify the subject, not to use their prose as a draft.
3. Record facts, source URLs, dates, limitations and unresolved questions in research notes.
4. Close or set aside source prose when drafting where practical; explain the subject from understanding, in Bagusin's own order and voice.
5. Add original synthesis: practical decision criteria, independently devised examples, steps, comparisons, limitations or lessons appropriate to the topic.
6. Compare the finished draft with important sources. Rewrite any wording, sequence, example or explanation that is too close.
7. Search the draft's distinctive phrases when useful, and use similarity tools as warning signals—not as guarantees.
8. If a section still depends on another writer's expression or unique structure, rewrite it from the underlying facts or remove it.
9. Keep a clear distinction between independently written analysis and sourced information.

Common facts and standard procedures may naturally resemble one another. Do not distort correct facts just to sound different. Originality comes from independently expressed explanation and responsible synthesis, not from changing facts or using awkward synonyms. Exact quotations, when genuinely necessary, must be brief, clearly marked, attributed and linked; do not use quotations to substitute for original writing. Observe applicable copyright and quotation limits.

## 3. Research and evidence standard

Use sources when claims depend on external information, especially:
- Statistics, numbers, survey results and market claims.
- Laws, regulations, official policies, eligibility rules and deadlines.
- Product/service features, platform requirements, prices, plans, limits and interface instructions.
- Technical specifications, security recommendations, compatibility and version-dependent behavior.
- Medical, financial, legal or other consequential claims (these need heightened care and qualified authoritative sources).
- Claims about what a named organization or person said or did.

Source hierarchy, adapted to the question:
1. Primary/official source: official documentation, government publication, original dataset, research paper, policy, product changelog or direct statement.
2. High-quality original research or reputable specialist source with transparent methodology.
3. Reliable secondary reporting that identifies its evidence.
4. Community posts and personal accounts only for clearly attributed experiences, not as sole proof of broad factual claims.

For example, use Google's official documentation for current Google product features or policies. Use a primary source for a quoted statistic when available. Do not cite a source merely because it mentions the same topic; verify that it actually supports the claim.

For each material external claim, research notes should capture:
- Claim or fact being used.
- Direct source URL and page title.
- Publisher/author where available.
- Publication/update date, or access date for undated pages.
- The exact portion of the source that supports the claim.
- Relevant limitations, context, geography, timeframe and uncertainty.

Link sources close to the claim they support when that helps readers verify it. Use descriptive anchor text rather than “click here.” A references/source section can supplement in-text links, but must not replace a needed link beside a specific statistic or policy. Cite direct sources rather than a page that merely repeats them whenever possible.

Not every ordinary sentence needs a citation. Cite when the source materially supports an externally derived or checkable claim. Clearly distinguish sourced facts from Bagusin's own interpretation or recommendation. Never fabricate citations, URLs, quotations, dates or supporting evidence. If a claim cannot be verified, remove it, qualify it honestly or state that it remains unverified.

External links and backlinks are not a reason to cite a page. Link because it is the relevant evidence or a genuinely useful resource for readers. Do not add irrelevant links to seek reciprocal backlinks or SEO benefit. Disclose affiliate or commercial relationships clearly whenever applicable, and do not let commission influence a recommendation.

## 4. The 50-article plan: map before drafting

Do not start by writing 50 articles one by one without a content map. First inspect the current repository and create or update a single article inventory/content map.

For each proposed article, record at least:
- Working title and stable content ID/slug.
- Primary reader and search intent.
- Main question answered and scope boundaries.
- Primary topic/query and natural supporting terms (not a keyword-stuffing list).
- Proposed category and tags/labels.
- Intended format and outline.
- What makes it meaningfully distinct from existing/planned articles.
- Existing related Bagusin pages and planned internal links.
- Relevant services, collections, learning paths or other next steps—only where genuinely useful.
- Expected sources/research needed.
- Status: idea, mapping, research, outline approved, draft, review, ready, published, or update needed.

Before approving an outline:
1. Search the existing site, repository, article inventory, categories and planned articles.
2. Compare each candidate with pages that target the same or closely related intent.
3. Decide whether it deserves a separate article, belongs as a section in an existing article, should be merged, or should be dropped.
4. Define a clear unique purpose, audience, main question and scope.
5. Assign one primary search intent and a coherent topic focus. Related terms are supporting language, not separate articles by default.
6. Plan how related articles complement one another and link to the appropriate pillar/supporting pages.
7. Approve the outline only after overlap and category/tag choices are reviewed.

Do not promise that two pages will never rank for overlapping terms; search behavior cannot be guaranteed. The practical goal is to prevent avoidable keyword cannibalization by giving each page a distinct purpose and keeping closely overlapping material together where that best serves readers.

## 5. SEO and article quality

SEO is a service to discoverability, not a formula. For each article:
- Identify the likely search intent: learn, perform a task, compare options, solve a problem, find an official resource, or make a decision.
- Check current search results and reliable sources when current SERP context materially matters. Do not copy competitors' headings or use their articles as the draft.
- Write a specific, accurate title and a useful meta description; avoid exaggerated promises.
- Use one clear H1 and a logical H2/H3 hierarchy. Headings should describe the actual sections and answer meaningful reader questions.
- Answer the core question early, then provide steps, context, examples, exceptions, troubleshooting or comparisons as appropriate.
- Include original, useful detail: decision criteria, worked examples, checklists, common mistakes, limitations or practical interpretation.
- Use the main topic and related language naturally. Never force exact-match keywords into every heading or paragraph.
- Add relevant internal links to complementary articles and useful pages, with descriptive anchors and without repeating links gratuitously.
- Add external links where they verify claims or help the reader continue to an authoritative resource.
- Use suitable images, diagrams or tables only when they add value; provide meaningful alt text for informative images and avoid unlicensed media.
- Check canonical URL, slug, language version, metadata, indexability and structured data against the site's current implementation when relevant. Do not add schema that misrepresents the page.
- For tutorials involving interfaces or features, verify steps against the current version and identify important version/date differences.
- Keep content scannable on mobile with clear paragraphs, useful lists and accessible tables.
- Do not invent expertise, authorship credentials, firsthand tests, review scores, testimonials or outcomes.

A good article is not automatically good because it is long. Cover the topic sufficiently, but remove repetition and sections that do not help the reader.

## 6. Categories, labels and taxonomy

Use the existing taxonomy as the source of truth; inspect the current site and content data before adding categories or tags.

- Categories represent stable, meaningful subject areas. Use the most appropriate existing category where possible.
- Tags/labels should connect articles that genuinely share a useful topic, not duplicate the category name without a reason.
- Do not create near-duplicate spellings, singular/plural variants, inconsistent capitalization or one-off labels without a clear editorial need.
- Do not assign a label just because a keyword appears once in the article.
- Check the category/tag pages, navigation and related-content behavior before adding or renaming taxonomy terms.
- If a new category or label is proposed, document its purpose and examples, then check the effect on existing articles and URLs.
- Keep Indonesian and English taxonomy aligned where the site supports both languages, while using natural labels in each language.
- Avoid changing published slugs or taxonomy URLs without a migration/redirect plan.

## 7. Internal links and content relationships

Each article should have a deliberate role in Bagusin's wider content:
- Link to a broader guide or pillar page when one exists and is genuinely relevant.
- Link to more specific follow-up articles when they answer the next question.
- Link to a service, collection/demo, course or consultation only when it is a natural next step for that reader.
- Avoid forcing a commercial CTA into a basic how-to article that does not need one.
- Use descriptive anchor text and check that each target exists, loads, and matches the promised destination.
- Review inbound/outbound relationships in the content map so new articles strengthen the library instead of creating isolated pages or repeated pathways.

## 8. Required workflow and review gates

### Gate A — Topic approval
Confirm audience, search intent, unique purpose, overlap audit, category/tags and intended internal links. No draft until the outline is distinct enough.

### Gate B — Research
Collect reliable sources and record evidence, dates, URLs, limitations and open questions. Decide which claims require citations. Do not draft unsupported numbers or policy claims.

### Gate C — Original outline
Build a fresh structure based on the reader's problem and the article's purpose. Do not mirror a competitor's distinctive heading sequence. Mark where source-backed claims, examples, steps and original analysis will appear.

### Gate D — Draft
Write from understanding in Bagusin's own voice. Explain the complete promised task. Do not pad for word count or repeat the same point under several headings.

### Gate E — Editorial and originality review
Check sentence-level copying, close paraphrase, translated copying, borrowed examples, overly similar structure, factual accuracy, source support, uncertainty, usefulness and tone. Rewrite or remove any suspect passages.

### Gate F — SEO, taxonomy and technical review
Check intent, title, meta description, heading hierarchy, slug, internal/external links, category/tags, related pages, mobile readability, image rights/alt text and technical metadata relevant to the site.

### Gate G — Publish decision
Publish only if every critical requirement passes. If facts, permissions, originality, or scope remain unresolved, return to research/draft or reject the article. Record status and update date in the content inventory.

### Gate H — Maintenance
Revisit articles when important facts, product interfaces, policies, source URLs or search intent change. Correct outdated information and links; preserve a truthful updated date and record substantial changes.

## 9. Publication checklist

- [ ] The article answers a defined reader question and has a distinct purpose.
- [ ] Existing and planned articles were checked for overlap and cannibalization risk.
- [ ] Outline and scope were approved before drafting.
- [ ] Wording, structure, reasoning, examples and presentation were independently created.
- [ ] No copied, closely paraphrased or translated source passage remains.
- [ ] Important external facts are supported by the right sources.
- [ ] Source links work and directly support the associated claims.
- [ ] Uncertain, time-sensitive and version-dependent claims are qualified or dated.
- [ ] Title, meta description, H1/H2/H3 hierarchy and search intent are appropriate.
- [ ] The content is complete for its stated promise, clear, concise and genuinely useful.
- [ ] Category, tags, slug and language version match the site's taxonomy.
- [ ] Internal links support related pages without forced promotion.
- [ ] No fabricated firsthand experience, statistics, citations, credentials or promises.
- [ ] Image rights, alt text, accessibility and mobile readability have been checked.
- [ ] Technical SEO details have been checked where applicable.
- [ ] Remaining concerns are resolved or the article is held back.
- [ ] Content inventory/status is updated.

## 10. Honest limitations

No human or automated tool can guarantee a universal “100% plagiarism-free” result, and search engines do not guarantee rankings. Bagusin's operational standard is to create every article independently, verify source-dependent claims, record evidence, review for similarity and duplication, and refuse publication when originality or accuracy is in doubt. Similarity checkers, search engines and AI detectors are supporting signals only—not certificates of originality.

## 11. Handoff rule for future ChatGPT sessions

When continuing work on Bagusin, first read `PROJECT-STATUS.md`, `ROADMAP.md`, this SOP, the existing article inventory/content map, and the latest relevant commits. Verify the current repository state before acting. Continue from the latest verified unfinished item, do not repeat completed work, and do not claim that research, originality checks, testing or publication happened unless it actually did.

The user should be able to continue with one short instruction:

**“Aya, baca catatan terbaru di repository Bagusin dan lanjutkan dari tahap terakhir yang benar-benar terverifikasi.”**

That instruction means: inspect the repository notes and relevant files, confirm the next unfinished task, follow the mandatory SOP and project rules, work in small verifiable steps, update the records, and report exactly what was done and checked.
