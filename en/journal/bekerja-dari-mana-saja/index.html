Working from anywhere does not mean working without direction

When the workplace can move, what needs to stay is the way decisions are made, rhythm is protected, and work gets finished.

    Remote work can look like complete freedom: open a laptop anywhere, keep a flexible schedule, and finish the work without going to an office. In practice, freedom without structure quickly becomes work that always feels unfinished.

    For me, working while moving is not about finding the most Instagrammable desk. What matters more is keeping the problem clear, agreeing on a realistic scope, and having a simple way to tell whether the work is actually moving forward that day.

    Direction matters more than location

    Location can change. Internet connections can slow down. A routine can break because of travel. That is why I try to begin with three things: what the problem is, what result needs to be finished, and what I am not going to work on yet.

    That last boundary sounds small, but it helps. Work often expands not because technical ability is missing, but because everything starts to look important at the same time.

    Working from anywhere still requires a clear decision about what needs to be done today.

    Build a portable rhythm

    I cannot always bring the same routine to every place. What I can bring is the sequence: understand the need, break the work down, handle the most important part, and communicate before a problem becomes larger.

    Start with the result, not the activity

    Instead of writing “coding for three hours,” it is more useful to define a result such as “the checkout flow can be tested from start to finish.” Activity becomes a tool for reaching the result, not the goal itself.

    Leave room for the unexpected

    Travel and work both come with surprises. Scheduling every minute means one small disruption can break the entire day. Empty space is not wasted time; often it is what keeps the plan realistic.

    When travel changes the plan

    Some days the work moves smoothly. Other days the plan has to change because of travel, connectivity, or a technical problem. The goal is not to protect the schedule perfectly, but to communicate clearly and reset priorities quickly.

    Project-based work also means being honest about capacity. If a request does not fit the agreed ability, time, or scope, it is better to say so early than accept it and disappoint someone later.

    Working note

    The workflow I use is simple: Problem → Requirement → Scope → Quotation → Payment/Deposit → Development → Review → Delivery.

    Freedom still means responsibility

    Working from anywhere does not mean living without structure. Because there is no office forcing a rhythm, I have to create that structure myself. The place can change, the process can evolve, and every project can be different. Responsibility for the result stays the same.

    That is what I want to document through BagusIn: not only places that look good, but the decisions, failures, work, and process of building something in between.
