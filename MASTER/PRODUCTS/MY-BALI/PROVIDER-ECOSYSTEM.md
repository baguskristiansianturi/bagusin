# My Bali — Provider Ecosystem and Verification

**Status:** Exploratory operating principles; detailed SOPs not yet approved

## 1. Purpose

My Bali is intended to connect users with the actual businesses and service operators responsible for the products or services being offered. The founder explicitly prefers direct providers over unverified resellers, brokers, lead sellers, or agents who merely insert themselves between the customer and the real operator.

## 2. Direct-provider principle

The preferred provider is:
- The owner or legally/operationally responsible business.
- An employee or representative who is genuinely authorized by that business.
- The actual operator responsible for delivering the listed service.

A person who is not the owner may apply only when their authority to represent the business can be established. Possible evidence includes a company authorization letter, appointment, or other verifiable mandate.

A reseller, broker, or intermediary should not be presented as the actual operator. Whether any narrowly defined authorized distribution arrangement may be permitted is still an open policy question; do not silently create exceptions.

## 3. Examples of provider types

Illustrative examples:
- A restaurant registering its own restaurant business.
- A watersport operator registering the actual watersport operation and facilities.
- A transport operator or vehicle owner registering services they are authorized to provide.
- An accommodation owner or authorized property manager registering the actual accommodation.
- A tour/activity operator registering the service it directly organizes and delivers.
- A shop or boutique registering its own business and products.

These examples do not establish the final eligibility rules for every industry.

## 4. Information and evidence to collect

A future onboarding SOP should define proportionate requirements, potentially including:
- Legal or trading identity of the business.
- Physical operating location and service coverage.
- Identity of the applicant.
- Applicant's relationship to the business.
- Evidence of ownership or authority to represent the business.
- Actual services/products and who fulfills them.
- Pricing, conditions, opening hours, capacity, and availability.
- Required business permits or industry-specific evidence where applicable.
- Contact and complaint-resolution channels.

Requirements must be appropriate to the provider category and applicable law. Avoid collecting unnecessary personal data.

## 5. Quality, reputation, and enforcement

Verification of identity does not guarantee good service quality. My Bali should separately monitor service reliability, customer feedback, complaints, policy compliance, and evidence of misconduct.

A proportionate enforcement process may include:
1. Receive and record a complaint or quality signal.
2. Assess evidence and distinguish a single disagreement from a repeated or serious issue.
3. Notify the provider and request clarification where appropriate.
4. Apply a documented corrective action or warning.
5. Restrict a listing or suspend relevant privileges when justified.
6. Remove a provider only under clear, consistently applied criteria, with an appeal/review process where appropriate.

The founder expects My Bali to provide substantial SOP guidance to providers and to control admission carefully. The exact standards, enforcement thresholds, and appeal process remain to be designed.

## 6. Reviews and reputation integrity

Reviews should relate to genuine experiences as far as the platform can reasonably establish. A verified booking or transaction can strengthen evidence of a commercial interaction, but it does not prove every statement in a review.

The platform should provide mechanisms to report suspected fake reviews, conflicts of interest, harassment, manipulation, and retaliatory behavior. Providers should not be able to suppress legitimate criticism merely because it is negative.

## 7. Fair marketplace principles

- Clearly identify the actual provider.
- Disclose commercial relationships and sponsored placement.
- Avoid presenting paid ranking as neutral recommendation.
- Make prices, exclusions, cancellation rules, and availability understandable.
- Do not imply real-time availability unless supported by reliable data.
- Apply provider standards consistently, including to any business affiliated with the founder.
